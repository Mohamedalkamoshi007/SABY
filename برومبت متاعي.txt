=== 4. WHAT WE DID AND LEARNED ===
1) Started with MAX4466 + internal ADC at 8 kHz. Fixed many wiring problems (floating GPIO, loose breadboard contacts, VCC once on 5V by mistake). 
   UDP streaming worked, but buzzing/noise remained because of ADC noise and supply ripple. Oversampling and denoising helped only partly.
2) Switched to INMP441 over I2S at 16 kHz: clean audio, no buzzing, packet loss below 2% on USB power.
3) Air-mic breathing tests (mic at 10/30/50 cm from nose, quiet breathing): breathing was NOT distinguishable from room noise (about 0 dB difference). 
   Conclusion: a mic placed in the air beside a baby's head cannot reliably capture quiet breathing, let alone wheezes or crackles.
4) Contact approach: funnel + tube on my chest. Heartbeat was clear (about 66 bpm estimated), and breaths were visible in the spectrogram as bursts around 400-1800 Hz 
   (clearest with mouth breathing after a ~5 s breath hold). Strong low-frequency rumble at 20-60 Hz (probably contact/movement). 
   In the nose recording there were fixed horizontal bands near 850 and 1250 Hz that may be tube resonance (long tube) and could be mistaken for wheezes; 
   plan: shorten the tube to 10-15 cm and compare. Lesson: spectrograms are a much better analysis tool than overall levels (my earlier level-based analysis was misleading).
5) I then bought a real stethoscope; I report the heartbeat is very clear and clean. I have not yet analyzed breathing or symptom recordings with it.
6) Battery incident (unresolved): on battery power through TP4056 + switch the audio had a pulsing artifact. When I added the 100 uF electrolytic cap across 5V and G 
   (grey stripe side = negative, intended on G), something got warm and the board LED did not light. Cause unknown (suspected short or reversed polarity). 
   Battery is now disconnected; USB only until the cause is understood. Safety: no battery or wires near a baby; test only on an adult.
7) Verdict agreed so far: the original idea (device beside the baby's head detecting wheeze/crackle live) will NOT work. 
   A contact (stethoscope-style) sensor on a person's chest can work as a research prototype: train a CNN on public data (e.g., ICBHI), 
   then test on my own recordings and report the accuracy honestly. Fallback if this fails: sound-event classification (cough, cry, heavy breathing, silence), with wheeze/crackle as an experimental extra. 
   The report must state: one subject, self-labelled (not clinician-confirmed), not a diagnostic device, not tested on infants.
